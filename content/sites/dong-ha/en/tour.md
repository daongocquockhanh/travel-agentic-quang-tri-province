## A town at a crossroads

> In the town centre

You are in Đông Hà, the main town of central Quảng Trị. It stands where National Route 1 meets the start of Route 9, the road that runs west to the Lao border at Lao Bảo. The town grew up at this river crossing and road junction, and that position has shaped its history.

## Built over a base

> On one of the main streets

During the war a large US combat base stood here. The town has since grown over it, and the old runway is now one of the main streets.

After 1975 Đông Hà developed into the provincial capital, until 1 July 2025, when Quảng Trị merged with Quảng Bình and the provincial centre moved to Đồng Hới. Route 9 is now part of the East–West Economic Corridor, linking central Vietnam with Laos and Thailand.

## Your base for the DMZ

> Planning your day

Most day trips to the old demarcation line start here: Vĩnh Mốc and Hiền Lương to the north, Trường Sơn and Khe Sanh to the west, La Vang and the Thạch Hãn to the south, and Cửa Việt for the boat to Cồn Cỏ. For the western sites, book a car with a driver or join a tour, because the distances are long.

## Eating your way through Quảng Trị

> At Đông Hà market, or along the Hiếu River

Đông Hà is a good place to taste Quảng Trị cooking. Look for cháo bột, a thick rice-noodle soup often made with fish or eel; bánh ướt cuốn thịt nướng, soft rice paper rolled with grilled pork and herbs; and small steamed cakes such as bánh lọc, bánh nậm and bánh bèo. Mai Xá mussel noodles, bún hến, are a regional favourite.

The market in the centre sells dried seafood, spices and local specialities to take home. In the evening, walk along the Hiếu River for street food.
