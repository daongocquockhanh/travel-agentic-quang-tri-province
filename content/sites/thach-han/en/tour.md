## The river

> On the bank by Quảng Trị town

This is the Thạch Hãn, the main river of central Quảng Trị. It flows past Quảng Trị town, where the old citadel stands on its bank, and reaches the sea at Cửa Việt. Its lower course is also called the Cửa Việt river.

On a quiet day it is simply a broad, green river. For many Vietnamese, though, it is above all a place of remembrance.

## The citadel on the bank

> Looking towards the citadel

In 1809 the Nguyễn court moved the provincial citadel of Quảng Trị to this spot on the Thạch Hãn, valuing it for administration, trade and defence. The citadel was first built of rammed earth, and later rebuilt in brick.

## 81 days and nights

> Facing the water

In 1972 the citadel and the town were the centre of heavy fighting. Vietnamese sources describe the defence of the citadel, from 28 June to 16 September 1972, as "81 days and nights". The area was subjected to intense bombing and shelling, and those sources state that thousands of soldiers died. Many crossed this river under fire.

The figures come largely from Vietnamese official sources and differ from other accounts. Soldiers on both sides were killed in the fighting for Quảng Trị that year.

## Flowers on the water

> At the wharf near the citadel

Releasing flowers and floating candles on the Thạch Hãn is a living memorial custom. It is often traced to April 1987, when a veteran of the 1972 battle bought all the flowers at the town market and let them float down the river for his fallen comrades.

Today ceremonies are held on major anniversaries, above all around 27 July, War Invalids and Martyrs' Day, when candles are released at dusk from this wharf. Visitors are welcome to join quietly.
