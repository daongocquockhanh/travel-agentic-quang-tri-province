In 1809 the Nguyễn court moved the provincial citadel of Quảng Trị to its present site on the Thạch Hãn, valuing the location for administration, trade and defence. The citadel was first built of rammed earth and later rebuilt in brick.

In 1972 the citadel and the town were the centre of heavy fighting. Vietnamese sources describe the defence of the citadel from 28 June to 16 September 1972 as "81 days and nights", during which the area was subjected to intense bombing and shelling, and state that thousands of soldiers died; many crossed the Thạch Hãn under fire. Figures for the battle come largely from Vietnamese official sources and differ from other accounts. Soldiers on both sides were killed in the fighting for Quảng Trị that year.
