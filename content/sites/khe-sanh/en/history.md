In August 1962 US Army Special Forces built a camp at an old French fort near Khe Sanh village, below Route 9, to watch movement along the border. US Marines deployed to the area in 1966 and manned the plateau base permanently from 1967, beside the airstrip.

The siege of Khe Sanh ran from 21 January to 9 July 1968. People's Army of Vietnam forces surrounded the base, which was supplied largely by air. In April an overland relief operation known as Operation Pegasus reached the base; the link-up took place on 8 April. The base was closed in July 1968.

Casualty figures for the battle differ widely between American and Vietnamese sources and remain disputed. The fighting cost many lives on both sides.
