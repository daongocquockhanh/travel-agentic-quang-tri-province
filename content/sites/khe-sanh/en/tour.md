## A plateau on Route 9

> At the entrance

You are on a plateau in the hills of western Quảng Trị, just south of Route 9, the road that runs on to the Lao border. The combat base that stood here was the scene of one of the longest and best-known battles of the Vietnam War, in 1968.

Today it is a museum and an open-air display, and a place of memory for veterans and families on all sides.

## From outpost to base

> In the museum

The story starts in August 1962, when US Army Special Forces built a camp at an old French fort near Khe Sanh village, below Route 9, to watch movement along the border.

US Marines deployed to the area in 1966. From 1967 they manned this plateau permanently, beside the airstrip you will walk along next.

## The siege

> Along the old airstrip

The siege of Khe Sanh ran from 21 January to 9 July 1968. Forces of the People's Army of Vietnam surrounded the base, and it was supplied largely by air, which is why this strip of ground mattered so much.

In April an overland relief operation, known as Operation Pegasus, reached the base. The link-up came on 8 April. The base was closed in July 1968.

## Remembering

> Among the aircraft and bunkers

Around you are a C-130 transport plane, a CH-47 Chinook helicopter, artillery and restored bunkers.

Casualty figures for the battle differ widely between American and Vietnamese sources, and remain disputed. What is certain is that the fighting cost many lives on both sides. Many visitors here are veterans or their families, so please take photographs thoughtfully and keep your voice low.
