Vinh Moc lay just north of the 17th-parallel demilitarised zone established by the 1954 Geneva Accords. From 1965 onward, the area came under sustained aerial bombardment as part of the war. To stay near their farms and fishing grounds rather than evacuate inland, residents began to dig. The first phase reached about 12 metres below ground; the second extended deeper to roughly 15 metres; the third descended to around 23 metres. The system grew to nearly 2 kilometres of passageways with thirteen entrances — seven facing the East Sea and six facing the hills — and contained ninety-four family compartments alongside shared facilities. Approximately sixty households, several hundred people in total, lived in the tunnels for about six years. Seventeen children were born underground during this period. The site was preserved after 1975 and recognised as a national historic relic in 1976.
