## A place of pilgrimage

> At the entrance to the grounds

You are about five kilometres southwest of Quảng Trị town, at La Vang, the most important Catholic pilgrimage site in Vietnam. The shrine honours Our Lady of La Vang.

La Vang is an active place of worship, and visitors of every background are welcome to walk the grounds. Please dress modestly, speak quietly, and don't photograph people at prayer or during Mass without permission.

## The story of 1798

> Walking through the grounds

According to Catholic tradition, in 1798 Catholics who had fled persecution were hiding in the forest here. They saw a vision of the Virgin Mary, who comforted them and told them how to treat the sick with leaves from the surrounding trees.

The shrine grew from that tradition, and successive churches were built on this site.

## The old bell tower

> By the old bell tower

A basilica was built here between 1924 and 1929. On 22 August 1961, Pope John XXIII designated the church a minor basilica.

During the fighting of 1972 the basilica was destroyed. Only this bell tower was left standing, and it has been preserved as it is.

## The new basilica

> Facing the new basilica

On 15 August 2012 the first stone was laid for a new basilica, in a traditional Vietnamese style and designed to hold about five thousand people. It is the largest construction project of the Catholic Church in Vietnam.

La Vang is busiest at the Marian feasts, above all around the Assumption on 15 August, when tens of thousands of pilgrims gather here.
