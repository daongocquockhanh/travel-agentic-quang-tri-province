According to Catholic tradition, in 1798 Catholics who had fled persecution and were hiding in the forest at La Vang saw a vision of the Virgin Mary, who comforted them and told them how to treat the sick with leaves from the surrounding trees. The shrine grew from that tradition.

Successive churches were built on the site. A basilica was built between 1924 and 1929, and on 22 August 1961 Pope John XXIII designated the church a minor basilica. During the fighting of 1972 the basilica was destroyed, leaving only the bell tower, which has been preserved.

On 15 August 2012 the first stone was laid for a new basilica, the largest construction project of the Catholic Church in Vietnam.
