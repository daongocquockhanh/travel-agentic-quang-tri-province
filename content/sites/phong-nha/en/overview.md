Phong Nha–Kẻ Bàng National Park covers a vast limestone karst landscape in the west of the province, close to the border with Laos. It is one of the world's great cave regions, with hundreds of caves and grottoes and more than a hundred kilometres of caves and underground rivers.

UNESCO first listed the park as a World Heritage site in 2003 for its geology and landforms, and again in 2015 for its biodiversity and ecosystems. In July 2025 it became part of the first transboundary natural World Heritage site in Southeast Asia, joined with Hin Nam No National Park in Laos.

Most visitors stay in Phong Nha town on the Son River, where boats leave for Phong Nha Cave. Paradise Cave and the forest roads of the park are a short drive away.
