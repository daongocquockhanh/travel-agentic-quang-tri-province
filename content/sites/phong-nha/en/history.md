The park's limestone formed over hundreds of millions of years, and rivers carved the caves through it. Phong Nha Cave is the best known: about 7.7 kilometres long, it carries an underground river that boats enter from the Son River, travelling the first stretch of the cave.

Many of the region's most famous caves were found by local people and surveyed with the British Cave Research Association. Hồ Khanh, a local man, found the entrance to Sơn Đoòng in 1990 and in 2009 led the British expedition that explored it; in 2013 Guinness World Records recognised Sơn Đoòng as the world's largest cave. He also found Paradise Cave (Động Thiên Đường) in 2005, a dry cave about 31 kilometres long that British cavers then explored and named.

UNESCO recognised the park in 2003 and again in 2015. In 2025 the listing was extended across the border to include Hin Nam No National Park in Laos.
