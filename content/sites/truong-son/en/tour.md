## The largest martyrs' cemetery

> At the gate

You are on the low hills beside Highway 15, about twenty-eight kilometres northwest of Đông Hà. This is the largest martyrs' cemetery in Vietnam: the resting place of more than ten thousand soldiers and volunteers of the People's Army of Vietnam who died along the Trường Sơn route, known in the West as the Ho Chi Minh Trail, and around the 17th parallel.

It is a place of mourning first. Families travel here from across the country to visit their relatives.

## Built after the war

> Walking up to the central memorial

Work began on 24 October 1975, a few months after the war ended, and was completed on 10 April 1977. The site was chosen in the Bến Tắt area, beside Highway 15, which had itself been part of the wartime Trường Sơn route.

The memorial stands at the centre of the hill. Visitors usually light incense here before walking among the graves.

## Zones by home province

> Among the graves

The graves are grouped into zones named after the soldiers' home provinces. As you walk, you will see the names of provinces from all over Vietnam.

There is also a section for soldiers who could not be identified. Many visitors leave flowers or incense there especially. Please walk on the paths, and do not step on or sit against the graves.

## 27 July

> Before you leave

The cemetery is busiest around 27 July, Vietnam's War Invalids and Martyrs' Day, when many families and delegations come and ceremonies are held.

Whenever you visit, you may see people mourning. Please ask before photographing them.
