A simple wooden bridge was first built here in 1928. In 1952 it was rebuilt as a seven-span bridge with concrete piers and steel beams, roughly 180 metres long. After the 1954 Geneva Accords fixed the Bến Hải as the temporary demarcation line, each side administered and painted its own half: the northern half blue and the southern half yellow, divided by a thin white line.

The two banks then competed through symbols. In what became known as the "flag battle", each side raised its flag ever higher; in 1962 the north bank flagpole reached 38.6 metres. Loudspeakers on both banks broadcast across the river in a parallel "loudspeaker battle".

The bridge was destroyed by bombing in 1967. It was restored to its original design in 2001, and the flagpole and border post have been rebuilt.
