## A river that divided a country

> At the south end of the bridge

In front of you, the Bến Hải River runs close to the 17th parallel. Under the Geneva Accords of 1954 it became the temporary military demarcation line between North and South Vietnam. For the next two decades, this bridge was the best-known symbol of a divided country.

## Blue and yellow

> Walking across the bridge

A simple wooden bridge first stood here in 1928. In 1952 it was rebuilt with seven spans, concrete piers and steel beams, about 180 metres long.

After 1954 each side administered and painted its own half: the northern half blue, the southern half yellow, divided by a thin white line. Walk across slowly. The change of colour at the middle is the clearest way to feel what this line meant.

## The flag battle

> Facing the flagpole on the north bank

The two banks also competed through symbols. In what became known as the flag battle, each side raised its flag higher and higher. In 1962 the flagpole on the north bank reached 38.6 metres.

At the same time, loudspeakers on both banks broadcast across the water, in a parallel loudspeaker battle.

## Destroyed and rebuilt

> On the north bank, by the old border post

The bridge was destroyed by bombing in 1967. It was restored to its original design in 2001, and the flagpole and the border police post were rebuilt.

The site was listed as a national relic in 1986, and as a Special National Relic in 2013. On the south bank, the exhibition house and the monument group take up the theme of the wish for reunification. Many people come here to remember family members, so please keep your voice low near the monuments.
